Abstract

Bringing object-centric learning into MLLMs — cheaply.

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have shown strong performance on Video Temporal Grounding (VTG). However, their coarse recognition capabilities are insufficient for fine-grained temporal understanding, making task-specific fine-tuning indispensable. This fine-tuning causes models to memorize dataset-specific shortcuts rather than faithfully grounding in the actual visual content, leading to poor Out-of-Domain (OOD) generalization. Object-centric learning offers a promising remedy by decomposing scenes into entity-level representations, but existing approaches require re-running the entire multi-stage training pipeline from scratch.

We propose SlotVTG, a framework that steers MLLMs toward object-centric, input-grounded visual reasoning at minimal cost. SlotVTG introduces a lightweight slot adapter that decomposes visual tokens into abstract slots via slot attention and reconstructs the original sequence, where objectness priors from a self-supervised vision model encourage semantically coherent slot formation. Cross-domain evaluation on standard VTG benchmarks demonstrates that our approach significantly improves OOD robustness while maintaining competitive In-Domain (ID) performance with minimal overhead.

01 Slot Adapter

Inserted only in the early decoder layers (1–7), the adapter projects visual tokens into a d=512 bottleneck, runs I=3 iterations of slot attention with K=4 learnable slots, then reconstructs the original token sequence via cross-attention. A zero-initialized up-projection makes the adapter an identity at the start of training — training stays stable while representations gradually become entity-level.

02 Slot Alignment Loss

Without supervision, slots may form arbitrary clusters. We align the token-pair similarity derived from slot attention weights with a DINOv2-derived affinity matrix, distilling objectness priors: 

LSA = 1 − ⟨ cos(Mslot), MDINO) ⟩

Combined with the standard cross-entropy: Ltotal = LCE + λ · LSA with λ = 0.1.
